## Problems that cut across pages

1. **"Convert X into Y."** The most common calque in both languages: μετατρέπει / μετατρέψτε σε (Greek, about 15 places), הופכים / להפוך ... ל (Hebrew, about 10 places).
2. **Greek Title Case.** Headings and buttons capitalise every word ("Δείτε Όλα τα Παραδείγματα"). Greek uses sentence case. Site-wide.
3. **One thing, many names.**
   - Each service has two or three different names (homepage card, services overview, service page). Example, Greek mobile-first: "Mobile-First Ανάπτυξη", "Με το κινητό στο επίκεντρο", "Ανάπτυξη με Προτεραιότητα στο Κινητό".
   - Care plans: Greek uses φροντίδα, συντήρηση, "φιλοξενία & φροντίδα" interchangeably.
   - Hebrew hosting: "אירוח" everywhere, but Israelis say "אחסון".
   - Hebrew WhatsApp: both "WhatsApp" and "וואטסאפ".
   - Greek brand gender: "η DM-Labs.io" on most pages, "το DM-Labs.io" on Pricing.
4. **" - " used as a dash.** Throughout Greek copy and some Hebrew. The brand rule is no em dashes.
5. **English leftovers in Greek:** custom, scope, launch, review, conversion-focused, SEO-ready, website visual pack, Enterprise / Custom, brand ecosystem.
6. **Mixed form of address in Greek:** mostly εσείς, but "Πάρε Οδηγίες" (Maps) and "σου" (Wix post).

## Facts that differ between languages (owner to confirm)

These are not wording problems. They need a decision before the batch that touches them. Nothing here has been changed.

1. **"Loved by 50+ businesses."** English and Greek Examples pages show 5 stars and "50+ businesses". Hebrew dropped the claim. If the number can't be backed up, remove it in all languages. The Greek wording is ungrammatical either way.
2. **Homepage testimonials** (Maria K., Andreas P., Sophia L.) appear in English and Greek, not Hebrew. If these are not real, attributable client quotes, EU consumer rules on fake reviews apply. Rewriting them would put words in people's mouths, so they are untouched.
3. **Greek Limassol page: "most websites delivered within 7 to 14 days."** The English Limassol page and every FAQ say the timeline is agreed per project.
4. **Thessaloniki: "One fixed price, everything included."** Also in English. It contradicts the mandatory hosting and care plan, taxes and separately agreed domain costs.
5. **Hebrew Maps page:** says the map "opens the Google Maps app" (Greek: "depends on device and settings") and that it "helps you appear in Google's Local Pack" with no "we don't guarantee placement" line (Greek has one).
6. **Greek city pages say a Greek version is "available on request",** and Nicosia and Thessaloniki say "this page is also available in English on request". Both are leftovers from translating the English pages.
7. **"Πρωτεύουσα της περιοχής" ("capital of the region")** on the Cyprus page looks like a side effect of the country-neutral rewrite (commit 9dbb20c, guarded by `server/internationalPositioning.test.ts`). Batch E needs to check what that test allows before fixing it.
8. **Known case from the brand refresh, Hebrew Growth page count:** resolved. All three homepages now use the shared package data (Growth: up to 4 pages).
